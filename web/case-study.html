01

Dataset — the raw materials

Eight CSV exports from different business systems: customers, catalog, inventory, suppliers, marketing, pricing, sales and returns. The transaction table is the fact core; the rest provide context for analysis.

Grain

One row per business key in reference data (customer, product, stock), one row per sales line in transactions, and one row per return. All monetary values are USD.

Coverage

36 months of history (2022-01→2024-12) across 500 products, 6 sales channels and 8,000 customers in multiple countries.

Quality concern

Raw exports preserve everything as text (dates, prices, booleans) — malformed values exist but are never dropped silently; they are flagged and surfaced in the cleaning layer.

Key cleaning rules

1
Source fidelity preserved

Bronze stores everything as VARCHAR — malformed dates and numbers are captured, never silently dropped.

2
Deterministic de-duplication

Keep the row with the highest bronze_id per business key (load order), since sources have no natural ordering column.

3
Type casting & standardization

Text converted to DATE, DECIMAL, INT; casing and whitespace normalized.

4
Business-rule validation

Order totals, margins and delivery lateness are recomputed from raw fields and compared with source values; mismatches are flagged, not propagated.

5
Orphan-key checks & reconciliation

Foreign keys validated (e.g. orders → customers, procurements → suppliers); row counts reconciled between Bronze and Silver.

Method notes

Revenue definition

Only completed and refunded transactions count as valid revenue — cancelled lines are excluded from revenue, profit and units.

Churn segmentation

Days since last order vs reference date 2024-12-31: ≤90 = Active, 91–180 = At Risk, >180 = Churned, no orders = No Orders.

Marketing attribution

Each channel’s reported revenue and ROAS is compared with the actual revenue from the sales fact table to expose the attribution gap.

Inventory health

days_of_supply = stock ÷ 90-day avg daily demand; status compares stock to reorder point and lead time.

05

Key insights

The findings that matter for the business — each one is backed by the computed metrics above.
INSIGHT 01

Dangerous category concentration

Electronics = 46.1% of all revenue ($23.2M of $50.2M). A supply shock, price war, or defect wave in one category moves the whole company — and its average margin is only ~20–21%.

INSIGHT 02

Strong, recurring seasonality

November–December drive the business: December alone = 13.1% of annual revenue and the top three revenue months are all Decembers. Clear planning signal for stock and marketing.

INSIGHT 03

Revenue is flat — no growth

Yearly revenue is effectively identical: $16.78M (2022) → $16.70M (2023) → $16.75M (2024). The engine runs, but it is not growing.

INSIGHT 04

Reported marketing revenue overstates reality

Platform-reported revenue is ~5.3% higher than actual sales ($52.9M reported vs $50.2M actual); per-channel gaps run 3.9–7.2%. ROAS actual = 13.2x vs reported 13.9x.

INSIGHT 05

41% of the customer base is slipping away

1,987 churned + 1,318 at risk = 3,305 of 8,000 customers. Each churned customer had real spend history — this is a large, low-cost re-activation pool.

INSIGHT 06

Margin varies wildly by category

Clothing runs high-margin items (a top seller shows 52.4% margin vs Electronics’ ~20%). Mix shifts toward Electronics lift revenue but compress gross margin.

INSIGHT 07

Inventory discipline is slipping

60 of 500 products (12%) sit at or below reorder point — with supplier lead times up to 28 days, demand can outpace supply at the wrong moment.

INSIGHT 08

7.5% return rate leaks $3.7M

7,126 returns refunded $3.7M. Even a 1-point reduction ≈ ~$0.5M recovered — likely rooted in the categories driving returns.
